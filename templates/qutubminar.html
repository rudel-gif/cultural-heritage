About Qutub Minar

Qutub Minar is a UNESCO World Heritage Site located in Delhi. It was built in the 12th century by Qutb-ud-din Aibak.


Standing at 73 meters tall, it is one of the tallest brick minarets in the world. The monument is famous for its Indo-Islamic architecture and historical importance.

Explore More About Qutub Minar

Qutub Minar is one of the tallest and most famous historical monuments in India.


Location of Qutub Minar

Qutub Minar is located in Mehrauli, Delhi, India. It is one of the most visited tourist attractions in the country and is surrounded by several ancient historical structures.


Who Built Qutub Minar?

Qutub Minar was started by Qutb-ud-din Aibak in 1192 after the defeat of the last Hindu kingdom in Delhi. Later, his successor Iltutmish completed the remaining floors of the tower.


History of Qutub Minar

The monument was built to celebrate the beginning of Muslim rule in India. Over the years, natural disasters such as earthquakes damaged some parts of the tower, but it was repaired several times by different rulers.


Architecture

Qutub Minar is made of red sandstone and marble and is designed in Indo-Islamic architectural style. The tower has beautiful carvings, Arabic inscriptions, balconies, and detailed patterns.


Main Attractions Inside the Qutub Complex

  • Qutub Minar Tower – The 73-meter tall tower is the main attraction.
  • Iron Pillar – A famous ancient iron pillar that never rusts.
  • Alai Darwaza – A beautiful gateway built by Alauddin Khilji.
  • Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque – One of the oldest mosques in India.

UNESCO World Heritage Site

UNESCO declared Qutub Minar a World Heritage Site in 1993 because of its historical and architectural importance.


Interesting Facts

  • Qutub Minar is 73 meters tall.
  • It has five different floors with balconies.
  • The tower contains verses from the Quran carved on its walls.
  • It is one of the tallest brick minarets in the world.
