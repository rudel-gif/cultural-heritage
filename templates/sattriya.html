Sattriya – Classical Dance

Sattriya is one of the eight classical dance forms of India and originated in the state of Assam. It was introduced by the great saint, scholar, and social reformer Srimanta Sankardev during the 15th century as a medium for spreading devotion and spiritual teachings.

The dance form combines graceful movements, expressive storytelling, devotional music, and drama. Sattriya performances often depict stories from the life of Lord Krishna, the Bhagavata Purana, and other Vaishnavite traditions.

Originally performed by monks in Vaishnavite monasteries called Sattras, Sattriya has evolved into a popular stage performance while preserving its spiritual and cultural essence.

History

Sattriya was created by Srimanta Sankardev and his disciple Madhavdev as part of the Neo-Vaishnavite movement in Assam. The dance was traditionally performed inside Sattras (monasteries) as a form of worship and religious expression. In the year 2000, Sattriya was officially recognized as one of India's classical dance forms.

Main Elements

Nritta: Pure rhythmic dance movements.

Nritya: Expressive dance conveying emotions and devotion.

Natya: Dance-drama based storytelling.

Bhaona: Traditional theatrical performances depicting religious stories.

Borgeet: Devotional songs composed by Sankardev and Madhavdev.

Costume and Music

Sattriya dancers traditionally wear costumes made of Assam silk, especially Pat and Muga silk. The music includes traditional instruments such as the Khol (drum), Cymbals, Flute, and devotional Borgeet songs.
