About Sanchi Stupa

Sanchi Stupa is one of the oldest stone structures in India and an important Buddhist monument located in Madhya Pradesh.


It was commissioned by Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE and serves as a symbol of Buddhist art and architecture.


The monument is famous for its beautifully carved gateways known as Toranas and its massive hemispherical dome.

Explore More About Sanchi Stupa

Sanchi Stupa is India's oldest and most famous Buddhist monument, showcasing the rich heritage of Emperor Ashoka's era.

Location

Sanchi is located about 46 km northeast of Bhopal in Madhya Pradesh.

History

The Great Stupa was originally built by Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE and later expanded during the Shunga period.

Architecture

The stupa consists of a hemispherical dome, central relic chamber, circumambulatory paths, and four magnificent gateways.

Main Attractions

  • Great Stupa No.1
  • Ashoka Pillar
  • Stone Toranas
  • Buddhist Monasteries
  • Sanchi Museum

UNESCO Heritage Site

Sanchi Stupa was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1989.

Interesting Facts

  • One of the oldest Buddhist monuments in India.
  • Built by Emperor Ashoka.
  • Contains relics of Buddhist monks.
  • Famous for intricate carvings depicting Buddha's life.
