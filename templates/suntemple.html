About Konark Sun Temple

The Konark Sun Temple is one of India's most famous historical monuments located in Odisha. Built in the 13th century by King Narasimhadeva I, it is dedicated to Surya, the Sun God.


The temple is designed in the shape of a gigantic stone chariot with twelve pairs of intricately carved wheels and seven horses.


It is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and is celebrated for its remarkable architecture and artistic excellence.

Explore More About Konark Temple

The Konark Sun Temple is considered one of the greatest achievements of Indian temple architecture.

Location

The temple is situated in Konark, Odisha, near the Bay of Bengal and about 35 km from Puri.

History

Built around 1250 CE by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga Dynasty, the temple symbolized the power and glory of the kingdom.

Architecture

The temple resembles a colossal chariot of the Sun God with 24 carved wheels and seven horses pulling it across the sky.

Main Attractions

  • Stone Chariot Design
  • 24 Decorative Wheels
  • Intricate Sculptures
  • Natya Mandapa (Dance Hall)
  • Sun Festival Celebrations

UNESCO Heritage Site

Konark Sun Temple was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.

Interesting Facts

  • The wheels of the temple can function as ancient sundials.
  • The temple was once called the Black Pagoda.
  • It represents extraordinary Kalinga architecture.
  • Millions of tourists visit every year.
