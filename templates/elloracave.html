About Ellora Caves

The Ellora Caves are one of India's most remarkable archaeological and cultural treasures located in Maharashtra.


The site consists of 34 magnificent rock-cut caves built between the 6th and 10th centuries.


These caves represent three major religions of ancient India: Buddhism, Hinduism, and Jainism, reflecting religious harmony and artistic excellence.

Explore More About Ellora Caves

Ellora Caves are famous worldwide for their breathtaking carvings and ancient engineering brilliance.

Location

The caves are located near Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad) in Maharashtra, India.

History

Construction took place between the 6th and 10th centuries under various dynasties including the Rashtrakutas.

Architecture

The caves were carved directly into basalt rock cliffs and display extraordinary sculpture work and architectural planning.

Main Attractions

  • Kailasa Temple (Cave 16)
  • Buddhist Monasteries
  • Jain Temples
  • Ancient Sculptures
  • Rock-Cut Pillars and Halls

UNESCO World Heritage Site

Ellora Caves were declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 because of their historical and cultural importance.

Interesting Facts

  • 34 caves are open to visitors.
  • The Kailasa Temple is carved from a single rock.
  • The caves extend over more than 2 kilometers.
  • They represent Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain traditions.
