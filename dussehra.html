Dussehra – Festival of Victory of Good over Evil

Dussehra, also known as Vijayadashami, is one of the most popular and widely celebrated festivals in India. It marks the victory of good over evil and is celebrated with great enthusiasm and devotion by people of all ages. The festival comes at the end of the nine-day celebration of Navratri and usually falls in the months of September or October according to the Hindu calendar. Schools, markets, and public places are decorated, and people wear new clothes and prepare special food to celebrate the occasion.

The main story behind Dussehra is related to Lord Rama from the ancient epic Ramayana. According to this story, Lord Rama fought a long battle against the demon king Ravana, who had kidnapped his wife Sita. After many days of fighting, Lord Rama finally defeated Ravana on the tenth day, which is celebrated as Dussehra. This victory symbolizes the triumph of truth, courage, and righte- ousness over evil and injustice.

On the day of Dussehra, people celebrate in many joyful ways. Large fairs called melas are organized in towns and cities. In many places, huge effigies of Ravana, Meghnath, and Kumbhkaran are made and burned in the evening to represent the destruction of evil forces. Cultural programs, dramas, and performances of Ramleela are also held to show the story of Lord Rama. Children enjoy fireworks, sweets, and games during the festival.

Dussehra has great cultural and moral importance in Indian society. It teaches people that good deeds, honesty, and truth will always win in the end. The festival also marks the beginning of preparations for another major festival, Diwali, which is celebrated a few weeks later. Therefore, Dussehra is not only a religious festival but also a celebration of hope, courage, and positive values in life.
