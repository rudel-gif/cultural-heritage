Makar Sankranti is the Festival of Harvest and Kites 🪁🌞

Makar Sankranti is one of the most popular festivals celebrated in India. It marks the transition of the Sun into the zodiac sign Capricorn, known as "Makara." The festival is celebrated every year on January 14 or 15 and represents the end of winter and the beginning of longer days. It is a harvest festival that brings joy and happiness to farmers and families.

On this day, people wake up early, take holy baths in rivers, and offer prayers to the Sun God. In many states, people prepare traditional sweets made from sesame seeds (til) and jaggery (gur). These sweets symbolize unity and warmth among people during the cold winter season.

One of the most exciting traditions of Makar Sankranti is kite flying. People of all ages gather on rooftops and open fields to fly colorful kites in the sky. Competitions are also organized, making the festival lively and fun. In different parts of India, the festival is known by different names such as Pongal in Tamil Nadu, Lohri in Punjab, and Uttarayan in Gujarat.

Makar Sankranti is a festival that celebrates nature, harvest, and togetherness. Families and friends share sweets, exchange greetings, and enjoy the festive atmosphere. The sky filled with colorful kites creates a beautiful and joyful scene across many cities and villages in India.
